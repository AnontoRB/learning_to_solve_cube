What is notation?

Notation is a short way to write cube moves. Each letter is one turn of one part of the cube. You read it from left to right and do the turns in that order. Once you can read it, you can learn any algorithm from any website, book or video.

RRight face, clockwise
UTop face, clockwise
R'Right face, counter-clockwise
U'Top face, counter-clockwise

That example is the “sexy move”. You will see it built step by step further down.

The six faces

Hold the cube in front of you. Each face has a letter, taken from its position, not from its colour.

U Up
the top face
D Down
the bottom face
F Front
the face toward you
B Back
the face away from you
R Right
the face on your right
L Left
the face on your left
The centres never move. Each face has one centre piece fixed to the core, so the centre colour tells you which colour belongs on that face. The pictures here use the common colours: white top, green front, red right, blue back, orange left and yellow bottom. Your cube may use a different scheme, and that is fine as long as you hold it the same way every time.

Face turns

A single capital letter turns that face a quarter turn (90°) clockwise, as if you were looking straight at that face. In every picture the bright pieces are the ones that move, the arrow shows the direction, and the right-hand cube shows the result.

Clockwise depends on where you look from. R and L are opposite faces, so when you look at each one from outside they turn in opposite directions on the cube. R moves the front pieces up, and L moves them down. Always imagine looking straight at the face you are turning.

Prime and 2

Two small marks change a turn. They work on every letter, so U', F2 and L' all follow the same rules.

Say R as “R”, R' as “R prime” and R2 as “R two”. Doing R and then R' puts the cube back exactly as it was.

Slice moves

The three middle layers have their own letters. Each one follows the direction of one outer face, which is easy to forget, so the picture and the rule are shown together.

M, E and S can also take a prime or a 2, for example M' or M2. Algorithms such as the H-perm and Z-perm use them.

Wide moves

A lowercase letter turns two layers at once: the face and the slice next to it. In some books it is written with a w, so r and Rw mean the same thing. They turn in the same direction as the capital letter.

Cube rotations

x, y and z turn the whole cube and do not change which pieces are connected. They only change how you hold it. Each one turns in the same direction as R, U and F.

When an algorithm starts with x, such as the Aa-perm, tip the whole cube first and then carry on with the next letter. Add a prime or 2 as usual: y', x2.

Reading an algorithm

Here is R U R' U' built one move at a time, starting from a solved cube. Compare each picture with the one before it.

  1. Read from left to right. Spaces separate the moves.
  2. Do each move exactly as written. A missing apostrophe is a different move.
  3. Brackets with a number repeat a group. (R U R' U')3 means do R U R' U' three times.
  4. Keep the same grip on the cube unless the algorithm has an x, y or z in it.
